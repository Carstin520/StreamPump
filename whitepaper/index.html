StreamPump connects creators, fans, and sponsors in one verifiable loop: content creation → creator momentum → fan participation → sponsor USDC budgets → on-chain settlement. Product workflow is DB-first; financial truth is chain-first on Solana.

SPUMP is non-transferable · never listed Sponsors are marketing spenders, not investors Content is the asset, not the token

Abstract

The creator economy is large and growing, but the layer it still lacks is trust: brands cannot verify whether a campaign actually happened, creators are paid flat fees with no structured upside, and fans have no way to participate beyond speculation. Web3's first attempts at this problem failed structurally — they were token-first products where view-to-earn emissions were dumped on exchanges, collapsing both the token and the community.

StreamPump takes the opposite stance. Content — not a coin — is the asset. The platform token, SPUMP, is a non-transferable Token-2022 utility token that is never listed on any exchange; it is the currency of conviction, voice, and reputation, earned through participation and spent on status and discovery, never sold. Sponsors fund creators with real USDC marketing budgets and receive verifiable on-chain campaign proof. Value moves directly between the people who create content and the people who fund it, settled on Solana.

This paper describes the StreamPump protocol: its two market seasons (creator discovery and sponsored campaigns), its three-track settlement model, the SPUMP utility and reputation economy, the Web2.5 architecture, and the compliance-aware constraints that shape every economic mechanism. Some layers described here are designed and not yet fully shipped; these are labeled explicitly throughout.

Why SocialFi 1.0 failed — structurally

Prior Web3 attempts at the creator economy collapsed for reasons that were structural, not cosmetic.

ProjectCollapseNote
friend.tech80k → <10k DAUrevenue fell to ~$71; contract abandoned
Farcaster15k → 545 new signups / mo$150M raised, then signups cratered
Lens37k → 142 new users / moa ~99.6% drop

Three recurring failure modes explain the pattern:

  1. Token-first. Teams launched coins instead of products; there was no sustainable content platform underneath.
  2. View-to-earn death spiral. Users earned a platform token, immediately sold it on a DEX, and the constant sell pressure crashed the price — then users left.
  3. Fake content ownership. Putting full content on-chain is slow and expensive, and creators do not value "data sovereignty" until their content already earns real revenue.

StreamPump inverts all three: it is product-first, its token cannot be sold, and it anchors attribution rather than claiming ownership.

02

Design Convictions

Five convictions shape every mechanism in the protocol.

1 · Content is the asset

Videos and posts retain audiences over time; meme coins and NFTs attract speculators. StreamPump does not tokenize or lock content — it anchors a creator-signed publication timestamp and an integrity fingerprint. That is attribution, not ownership: enough to route revenue to the right creator, nothing more.

2 · SPUMP is utility-only

Non-transferable, never listed on any DEX or CEX. Users earn USDC through in-platform participation and sponsor-funded outcomes — never by trading a token on secondary markets.

3 · Sponsors are spenders

Sponsors run marketing campaigns and pay in USDC — the same budget they already deploy elsewhere. This is a far healthier source of capital than speculation, and it is the protocol's primary inflow.

4 · Automation-first economics

The protocol is highly automated. Service fees and small USDC transaction fees sustain it — no extractive token tax, no dependence on token-price appreciation.

5 · DB-first for workflow, chain-first for financial truth Drafts, uploads, content manifests, proposal intents, and retry state live in the database, where they are fast and flexible. Funding, token movement, content anchoring, settlement, and claimable value are treated as Solana truth. This single split is what separates StreamPump from a fan-token casino on one side and a slow, expensive "everything on-chain" product on the other.
Status is the headline reward; USDC is a bounded bonus The protocol is designed on the assumption that the durable driver of participation is status, identity, access, and curation reputation — not financial upside. Early users will include money-motivated participants, but the model does not depend on them. Any USDC a backer receives is a capped, sponsor-funded discovery reward, never a return that scales with how much they staked.

Protocol Overview

StreamPump places content creation, creator momentum, fan participation, sponsor budgets, and on-chain settlement into one product loop, with three interacting roles.

Three roles, one exchange

RoleContributesReceives
CreatorContent, momentumCold-start backing, then structured sponsorship payouts (base + performance)
Fan / BackerNon-transferable SPUMP, curation, early convictionPermanent founding status, reputation, and a capped, sponsor-funded discovery reward
SponsorUSDC marketing budgetDirect creator access (no agency) and verifiable on-chain campaign proof

The protocol runs in two seasons. Season 1 is the creator discovery market, where early conviction is expressed. Season 2 is the sponsored campaign market, where structured commercial value flows.

04

Season 1 — Creator Discovery Market

S1 is how early creators bootstrap commercial value. A fan burns non-transferable SPUMP and receives an internal S1 position — recorded as a program-derived account (PDA) on Solana, not a tradable token. There is no creator SPL token and no secondary market; an S1 position is internal protocol state.

The position price follows a rating-adjusted quadratic bonding curve. An oracle evaluates the creator's momentum — followers, views, growth velocity, and StreamPump-native engagement signals — and adjusts a rating that scales the curve. (Curve, rating, and reward formulas are in the appendix.)

This is conviction-based discovery, not a fan-token market SPUMP is unbuyable: it can only be earned through genuine, time-based participation. Spending it to back a creator is therefore a credible costly signal of real conviction — which is exactly what makes backing a trustworthy momentum signal instead of buyable hype. The trust-layer thesis depends on SPUMP being unbuyable.

Anti-speculation guardrails

  • Non-transferable token. SPUMP is a Token-2022 mint with the NonTransferable extension — enforced at the protocol level, not by social convention.
  • Daily buy caps. Per-user, per-creator caps prevent any single actor from flooding a curve.
  • Dynamic exit tax. Selling ("unbacking") carries a decaying exit friction that discourages churn and front-running.
  • Delayed rating activation. Rating changes take effect after a delay, preventing front-running of momentum updates.
  • Early-cohort buyout cap. The share of a future buyout attributable to the earliest cohort is bounded.
  • Loyalty-gated capacity DESIGNED. The daily backing cap scales with the fan's loyalty tier for that creator, so backing power is a function of demonstrated loyalty and time, not capital.
05

S1 Buyout & Graduation

When a creator reaches critical mass, the buyout mechanism bridges S1 conviction into S2 commercial value. Sponsors submit USDC buyout offers (optionally with requirements, e.g. "the next three ad slots"); multiple sponsors can compete.

Once a creator accepts, a rage-quit window opens: any backer who dislikes the deal can exit at full curve price with zero exit tax. After the window closes, graduation executes — the creator moves to S2, and the buyout USDC is distributed.

Economics & reward funding

This is the part designed most carefully, because it determines whether backing reads as an investment or as a reward for an activity.

  • Source of funds. The discovery reward pool comes primarily from the sponsor's committed commercial budget (the buyout USDC) — money the sponsor was already spending on creator discovery and acquisition. Optional platform top-ups exist as an early-growth / promotion tool, but are not a core economic assumption; the protocol does not depend on subsidizing rewards.
  • The creator gets the majority. The bulk of the buyout USDC is paid directly to the creator as commercial proceeds (a configurable creator share). It is the creator's deal.
  • Backer reward is capped and decoupled. The remainder funds a backer discovery-reward pool that is distributed by a non-proportional rule — by eligibility, earliness, or loyalty tier — with a hard per-user cap. It is never a pro-rata slice scaled by how much SPUMP a backer staked.
  • Status is the headline reward. The permanent "founding supporter" status and reputation are the primary payoff for early backing; USDC is a bounded bonus.
Why the design is shaped this way Decoupling the reward from stake size, capping the upside, and routing the bulk to the creator are deliberate: they recharacterize the backer payout as a reward for the activity of early curation rather than a return on capital. See §11, Compliance-aware Design.
06

Season 2 — Sponsored Campaign Market

S2 is where structured commercial value flows. A sponsor funds a creator campaign through a three-track budget model that gives granular control while protecting creators and rewarding fans.

TrackModelSettlement logic
Track 1Fixed base payUnconditional creator payout — the "you definitely get paid" floor.
Track 2Performance budgetCliff threshold: below it, the sponsor is fully refunded. Above it, achieved budget splits 80% creator / 20% into a fan endorsement pool. Fans burn SPUMP to endorse; rewards are capped and flat, not stake-proportional.
Track 3 GATEDCPS (cost per sale)Sponsor pre-funds; settles after a refund window. Unused balance returns to the sponsor. Remains gated until a real merchant / reconciliation provider exists — the protocol does not fabricate sales data.

Campaign lifecycle

Content manifest → proposal intent → creator signs → sponsor signs & funds → Solana vault → per-track settlement. Each campaign produces a public, verifiable proof — proposal PDA, transaction signatures, content-anchor hash, funding and settlement state — that anyone can audit. This is the deliverable a sponsor cannot get from a Web2 agency.

07

SPUMP Utility Model

SPUMP is the platform's native conviction / voice / energy currency — not "money you earned but cannot withdraw." Nobody resents being unable to sell game energy, a vote, or forum karma, because those were never money. Non-transferability is the feature: because SPUMP can only be earned through genuine participation, spending it is a credible signal.

Earning PARTIAL

  • Daily SPUMP claim (transaction-wired)
  • Engagement rewards for genuine activity
  • Emission decays from 10× to 1× as the network grows, keeping SPUMP scarce

Hard boundaries

  • Non-transferable (Token-2022)
  • No token sale, ever
  • Never listed on any DEX / CEX
  • No per-SPUMP redemption, no stake-proportional USDC claim, no yield multiplier, and no SPUMP price expectation

SPUMP sinks DESIGNED

Always-available sinks decouple SPUMP demand from "is there a graduation-worthy creator to back right now." Every creator a user follows becomes a place to spend SPUMP today — on voice, identity, reach, relationship, and reputation.

SinkEffect
Badge tier claim / upgradeTurns earned eligibility (duration + engagement) into a visible status artifact. Time gates the floor; a SPUMP burn claims the tier.
Cheer / 打赏A costly social signal that strengthens a creator's momentum (feeds the oracle's engagement-quality input) and anchors a milestone.
Content boost / 助推Signal-weighted, decaying boost to feed ranking; capped to prevent pay-to-rank.

The line we never cross SPUMP can buy voice, identity, reach, relationship, and reputation. It can never buy a yield multiple: no sink converts SPUMP to USDC, raises a USDC claim, raises a buyout share, or manufactures a SPUMP price expectation. Making most SPUMP demand non-monetary utility is also what collapses any implicit USD price for the token.
08

Platform Level & Scout Reputation

DESIGNED StreamPump separates standing into two orthogonal axes, so that a user's influence reflects both seniority and the quality of their judgment.

Level (Lv0–Lv6)

A sticky measure of seniority and trust — how long and how genuinely a user has participated. It rises with honest activity and is hard to fake or buy.

Scout reputation

A slashable, outcome-validated measure of curation skill: Passerby → Observer → Scout → Gold Scout (路人 → 观察者 → 星探 → 金牌伯乐). It rises when the creators you backed early actually succeed.

The two compose into a sublinear, capped Influence Weight. Higher-standing users' likes, cheers, and endorsements allocate more discovery traffic and contribute more to displayed creator momentum. Anti-plutocracy rules apply: a universal base weight, a sublinear cap, and an earliness path so newcomers can still matter.

The compliance firewall Influence is a reputation and discovery currency, never a financial one. It moves traffic, ranking, and displayed momentum freely — but it never multiplies USDC, price, or claims. It can reach a creator's valuation only as one bounded input to the oracle's existing, guarded rating path. Influence = reach + reputation, never earnings.
09

Settlement & Metric Verification

Financial outcomes are settled on Solana from PDA-owned vaults, idempotently. Track 1 settles unconditionally; Track 2 settles against a verified metric at a cliff; Track 3 settles after a refund window against approved sales. The integrity of Track 2/3 depends entirely on the quality of the metric source.

Target metric architecture DESIGNED — not yet integrated

The protocol does not yet integrate a production metric or merchant provider; performance settlement currently runs against controlled data, and Track 3 is gated. The intended architecture, stated plainly so the risk is not hidden:

  • Platform API / creator-connected accounts — metrics sourced from the creator's own connected platform accounts, not scraped third-party numbers.
  • Signed metric attestations — an oracle signs an evidence digest of the metric snapshot, recorded on-chain.
  • Fraud scoring — anomalous engagement is flagged and can hold settlement.
  • Evidence digest + delayed settlement — settlement references a digest and occurs after a window.
  • Dispute window — sponsors and creators can contest a settlement before it finalizes.

StreamPump-native engagement (costly cheers and boosts paid in earned SPUMP) is itself a hard-to-fake signal that complements external metrics and feeds the creator-momentum rating.

10

Architecture — Web2.5 by design

StreamPump is deliberately hybrid. The single most important architectural principle — and the clearest line separating it from a fan-token product, a creator CRM, or a view-to-earn farm — is the split between workflow and financial truth.

Why Solana

Each core mechanism depends on a specific Solana capability:

  • Token-2022 NonTransferable. SPUMP is soulbound at the protocol level — yet Token-2022 still permits Burn, so "burn SPUMP to back a creator" works natively, with no workaround.
  • Low-latency confirmation & low fees. Fast confirmation and low per-transaction cost make frequent settlement interactions — bonding-curve trades and Track 2/3 micro-settlements — practical, which is impractical on a high-fee L1.
  • PDA composability + Anchor. Protocol config, creator profiles, S1 positions, proposals, and USDC vaults are deterministic, composable on-chain accounts in a single type-safe Anchor program covering the full S1/S2 lifecycle.
11

Compliance-aware Design

StreamPump is designed with compliance-aware constraints. This section describes design choices intended to reduce regulatory risk; it is not a legal conclusion, and nothing here states that any token or mechanism is "compliant." Final characterization requires qualified counsel.

The central concern is that a structure of the form "spend a cost-bearing input to back a creator early, then receive USDC scaled by how much you staked when a sponsor buys them out" resembles an investment contract. The protocol's economic design deliberately breaks that pattern, in depth:

  • Economic substance first. Backer USDC is decoupled from stake size and hard-capped per user. More SPUMP backed never buys more USDC. The bulk of buyout USDC goes to the creator; the backer portion is a capped, sponsor-funded discovery reward for the activity of early curation — the same category as a rebate or finder's reward, not a return on capital.
  • Token framing. SPUMP is a non-transferable consumption unit with no sale and no listing. Most SPUMP demand is non-monetary utility (cheer, boost, badges), which collapses any implicit USD price.
  • Access controls. Initial rollout is geofenced, with KYC for users who can receive USDC and KYB for sponsors.
  • The influence firewall. Reputation and influence move discovery and reach freely but never multiply a financial claim.
  • Narrative discipline. Product and docs avoid "investment," "ROI," "yield," "returns," and SPUMP price charts; rewards are described as discovery / loyalty / supporter rewards.
Standing blockers before any real-money launch A token-classification legal opinion, a first-launch jurisdiction decision, and an independent audit of the settlement program are required and cannot be self-certified by the team. The reward-decoupling redesign described above is implemented at the code level and remains gated behind these reviews.
12

Go-to-Market

StreamPump is a three-sided market — creators, fans, sponsors — so cold-start sequencing matters more than any single feature. The strategy is to make one narrow corridor real before widening.

  • Creator-first supply. Initial rollout prioritizes a narrow creator vertical where conviction-based discovery is natural, building a credible supply of creators and verifiable campaign case studies before scaling demand.
  • Sponsors that fit early. The earliest sponsors are likely crypto-native or innovation-friendly brands comfortable with on-chain settlement and verifiable proof.
  • Jurisdiction selection. Rollout favors jurisdictions where the compliance-aware reward design can be validated before broader expansion.
The honest GTM risk: sponsor adoption Convincing mainstream brands to fund on-chain campaigns is the hardest part of this model, and the paper does not pretend otherwise. The mitigation is treasury abstraction: sponsors should pay in fiat, with the platform or a payment partner handling USDC on-ramp and custody. A sponsor sees a budget, a campaign, and a settlement receipt — not wallets, gas, or RPC. On-chain verifiability is a back-office property of the settlement, not a UX burden placed on the buyer.

StreamPump is an advanced prototype. The first production corridor is verified end-to-end on devnet: an authenticated creator publishes content through R2/Mux, the post appears in the public feed from a backend projection, the creator opens a proposal intent, creator and sponsor co-sign, the backend relays the transaction to Solana, and the campaign detail shows verifiable proof — real proposal PDA, confirmed relay, manifest hash, funded state.

Appendix — Formulas

Reference formulas for the mechanisms described above. Parameters live in on-chain ProtocolConfig and are tunable without changing PDA seeds; default values are illustrative and subject to audit.

A.1 · Rating-adjusted bonding curve (S1)

The S1 position price follows a quadratic curve scaled by the creator's momentum rating. With supply S, trade size dS, base coefficient base_k, and rating in basis points:

effective_k = base_k × creator_rating_bps / 10_000
buy_cost   = effective_k / 2 × ((S + dS)² − S²)
sell_return = effective_k / 2 × (S² − (S − dS)²)

Rating defaults to 10_000 (1.0×) and is bounded to 5_000–20_000 (0.5×–2.0×), with a per-day change cap and delayed activation to prevent front-running of momentum updates.

A.2 · Buyout split & capped discovery reward (S1)

At graduation the sponsor's committed buyout USDC (D) is split between the creator and a bounded discovery pool. Backer rewards are capped per user and decoupled from stake size — distribution depends on holder counts and tier, never on SPUMP staked.

creator_payout = D × creator_share_bps / 10_000
discovery_pool = D − creator_payout
# flat model
reward(user) = min( discovery_pool / eligible_holders, per_user_cap )
# earliness-tiered model (early weight w_e, regular w_r)
unit = discovery_pool × w / (early·w_e + regular·w_r)
reward(user) = min( unit, per_user_cap )

Any unclaimed residual after the claim window is swept to the configured destination (creator or sponsor); it is never re-distributed proportionally to backers.

A.3 · Track 2 settlement & capped fan reward (S2)

Track 2 settles against an oracle-validated metric at a cliff threshold. With deposited budget B, achieved fraction a (capped at 1.0), and minimum cliff c:

if a < c:  sponsor_refund = B  (creator = 0, fans = 0)
else:
  achieved   = B × a
  creator_pay = achieved × 80%
  fan_pool   = achieved × 20%
  sponsor_refund = B − achieved
  # capped flat, not stake-proportional
  reward(fan) = min( fan_pool / endorser_count, per_user_cap )

Failed-campaign endorsements refund 100% SPUMP principal; cancelled/voided campaigns refund principal minus a fixed slash. Track 3 (CPS) settles after a refund window against approved sales and remains gated until a real merchant provider exists.
