## Slide 1 — Where it started (~50s)

So this whole thing started with an interview — 罗永浩 sitting down with Tim, the guy behind 影视飓风. Around 30 million followers across platforms — one of the biggest creators in China.

And listening to it, one thing really hit me: the way creators get funded today just doesn't work — at any level.

Think about where you start. If you're a beginner, brands won't even look at you until you've got, what, around a million followers. Below that, to them, you basically don't exist.

Okay, so say you make it — you're a mid-sized creator now, you've got the audience. You still end up handing over most of your margin — I'm talking forty, fifty percent — just to prove you can actually sell.

And then there's the top. Someone like Tim, sixteen million followers. His problem isn't survival, and it isn't money. It's that traditional VC just doesn't fit a content company — throwing in more cash and more people doesn't make better videos, and it doesn't make him worth more. What he actually needs is a way to take his IP, build it into a brand, and keep growing his audience. Because for a creator, that's the real asset — the attention, the audience. Not the cash.

So that's what StreamPump is really about. Whether you're just getting started or you're already huge — give creators a healthier way to grow that audience, and actually get paid for it.

And crypto already tried to fix creator money. It mostly died. Friend.tech went from 80,000 daily users to nearly zero — revenue dropped to 71 dollars. Lens and Farcaster fell off the same cliff, even after raising hundreds of millions. Yet the creator economy is worth 480 billion dollars, and still has no trust layer between creators, fans, and sponsors. That's the gap.

## Slide 3 — Three reasons they died. We flipped all three. (~40s)

So why did all of them die? It really comes down to three things — and on each one, we did the opposite.

First, the token itself. Their way: launch a tradable coin, and let speculators pump it and dump it. What we did instead — SPUMP is soulbound. Non-transferable, never listed anywhere. And the logic is simple: if there's no market to trade it on, there's nothing to speculate on. It's a reputation token, not a casino chip.

Second, where the money actually comes from. Their way: print an inflationary token to pay users — which sends the price into a death spiral. What we did instead — we fund the vaults up front, with real brand budgets, in USDC. The logic there: the yield has to come from real commercial spenders, not from a money printer.

And third, the content. Their way: shove entire videos on-chain — slow, expensive, kind of pointless. What we did instead — videos stay in a fast database, and we only anchor a signature and a hash on Solana. So: Web2 speed for the workflow, Web3 truth for the money.

That's the whole stance in one line — on StreamPump, sponsors are marketing spenders, not token gamblers.

## Slide 4 — The product: one loop, three roles (~30s)

StreamPump sits between Web2 and Web3 — we call it Web2.5. The feel of a normal app, with real settlement underneath.

The loop: content, creator gets hot, fans back them early, sponsors pay in USDC, Solana settles.

Three roles. Creators get their first money from fans, then real deals with guaranteed pay plus upside. Fans back a creator early and earn USDC when that creator gets bought out. Sponsors skip the agency, pick a budget, pay in USDC, get proof on-chain.

Now let me show you how each piece actually works.

## Slide 5 — S1: how fans back a creator (~40s · animated)

Season 1 is discovery. Here's the move on screen: a fan burns SPUMP — and gets a position in the creator.

Two things matter. SPUMP can't be transferred. And that position isn't a tradable creator coin — it's just protocol state on-chain. So there's no token to pump, no token to dump.

The price follows this curve on the right. And an oracle watches the creator — followers, views, growth — and scales the curve as they get hotter.

And the guardrails are built in: daily buy caps, an exit tax, delayed ratings. You're backing conviction, not flipping a coin.

## Slide 6 — The buyout: auction, money flow, fan split (~50s · animated · key)

This is the bridge, and it's the part people care about — how does anyone actually get paid.

When a creator gets big enough, sponsors compete. Watch the left: three sponsors bid USDC, and the winning bid gets escrowed into a vault on-chain.

Then the split — watch the right. The creator takes the majority. That's the point: we want the creator to win.

The fans who backed them early also get paid — but look closely. There are just two tiers: early backers and regular backers. Early backers earn twice what a regular backer does — two to one — and every payout is capped, about a hundred dollars max. It's based on how early you were, not how much you staked. Inside a tier, a whale and a small fan get the exact same amount. That kills the gambling, and it keeps us on the right side of the line legally.

And if a fan doesn't like the deal? Before the deadline, they rage-quit — full price back to SPUMP, zero exit tax. Nobody's trapped.

So the full loop pays off: back a creator early, a sponsor pays real USDC to buy them out, and you get a real cut.

## Slide 7 — S2: how creators take sponsorship (~45s · animated)

Once a creator graduates, this is the real business — Season 2. A sponsor brings USDC, picks a track, the vault settles. Three ways to work together.

Track 1, fixed. Guaranteed base pay — the bar fills all the way, unconditional. You definitely get paid.

Track 2, performance. The budget is tied to real results — views, clicks, saves. There's a cliff line: below it, the sponsor gets a full refund; above it, eighty percent to the creator, twenty into a capped fan pool. So fans who endorsed the campaign share the upside too.

Track 3, cost-per-sale. The sponsor pre-funds, we wait out a refund window, then settle to the creator. Whatever's unused goes back to the sponsor.

One menu — guaranteed, performance, or per-sale. The sponsor controls the risk; the creator and the fans share the reward.
